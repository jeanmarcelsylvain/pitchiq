# User Personas — PitchIQ

---

## Persona 1: The ECNL Competitor

**Name:** Maya Chen  
**Age:** 17  
**Position:** Center Midfielder  
**Level:** ECNL National  
**Location:** San Jose, CA

### Background
Maya trains 5 days a week with her ECNL team and has been playing competitive soccer since age 8. She is actively pursuing a Division I college scholarship. She watches professional soccer, follows analytics on social media, and uses Strava to track her runs. She is tech-savvy and metrics-oriented.

### Goals
- Earn a D1 scholarship
- Stand out to college coaches with quantifiable development
- Track whether specific training investments are paying off in matches

### Pain Points
- No centralized place to track her individual stats across tournaments and league matches
- College coaches ask about specific metrics (pass accuracy, sprint speed) and she has to estimate
- She can't see whether her off-season training is actually translating to match performance

### How PitchIQ Helps
PitchIQ becomes Maya's performance journal. After every match, she logs in 45 seconds. By mid-season, she has a shareable stats profile to send to college coaches alongside her highlight video.

### Quote
*"I work so hard in training. I just want to know if it's actually showing up in games."*

## Persona 2: The High School Varsity Player

**Name:** Jordan Williams  
**Age:** 16  
**Position:** Striker  
**Level:** High School Varsity (State qualifier)  
**Location:** Austin, TX

### Background
Jordan is a natural scorer who plays for his high school and a local club team on weekends. He's not sure whether to pursue college soccer but wants to take his game seriously. He uses TikTok for soccer tips and is inspired by Premier League highlights. He doesn't currently track any stats.

### Goals
- Improve his goal-scoring record this season
- Get better feedback than "you played well" from his coach
- Have something concrete to show for all the hours he puts in

### Pain Points
- His high school coach doesn't keep detailed individual stats
- He doesn't know if he's improving or just playing the same way season after season
- He has no way to set measurable goals for himself

### How PitchIQ Helps
Jordan uses the Goals page to set a target of 20 goals for the season. The progress bar keeps him motivated. He discovers through analytics that his performance rating drops in second halves — so he starts focusing on fitness. By season's end he can quantify his improvement.

### Quote
*"I want to see actual numbers, not just vibes."*

## Persona 3: The Recreational Player Leveling Up

**Name:** Sofia Ramirez  
**Age:** 20  
**Position:** Left Winger  
**Level:** College Club / Recreational League  
**Location:** Chicago, IL

### Background
Sofia played high school varsity but chose a college with no varsity program. She plays for the college club team and a Sunday recreational league. She exercises consistently and considers herself a committed hobby player. She uses fitness apps (Nike Run Club, MyFitnessPal) and is comfortable tracking personal metrics.

### Goals
- Stay in shape and keep soccer in her life during college
- Get better at passing, which she knows is a weakness
- Have fun with a product that feels modern and personal

### Pain Points
- Recreational leagues have no stats tracking whatsoever
- She doesn't know what to work on without objective data
- Fitness apps don't cover sport-specific performance

### How PitchIQ Helps
Sofia logs casually — a few matches per month — but uses the Analytics page to watch her pass accuracy trend upward over a semester. It becomes a motivational tool even for a non-elite player. She recommends it to teammates.

### Quote
*"I just want something that makes me feel like I'm being intentional about getting better."*
